1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This document specifies the requirements for VisionGuard, an Android application that
protects users' eyesight, screen privacy and time using on-device computer vision and
local usage tracking. It is intended for developers, testers, evaluators and stakeholders.

1.2 Scope
VisionGuard is a privacy-first phone safety and wellbeing system. It combines existing
technologies (on-device face detection, Android usage statistics, overlay windows) into
one integrated, resource-efficient experience. It does not introduce new AI models. All
processing is local; the prototype has no cloud component.
In scope: Eye Guard, Privacy Guard, Smart Dashboard, Multi-User Profiles, Time Tokens,
local encrypted storage. Out of scope (prototype): cloud sync, remote parental control,
face-based user identification, iOS support, content filtering.

2. Overall description
2.1 Product perspective
VisionGuard is a standalone Android application. It relies on the device front camera, the
Android usage-statistics service and the system overlay facility. It is independent of
Google accounts and any server.

2.5 Design and implementation constraints
C1: Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, MVVM, Hilt, Coroutines/Flow.
C2: The camera cannot be accessed from a plain background service. A camera-type
foreground service with a persistent notification is mandatory (Android 14 requires the
FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CAMERA permission).

C3: Android 12+ blocks touch pass-through for overlays above 0.8 opacity, so the dim
effect is capped at 0.8.
C4: Blur-behind is device-dependent; a frosted/dark overlay is the fallback.
C5: Android's native multi-user is not available to normal apps, so profiles are app-level.
C6: Some OEM skins (Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung) may terminate background
services.
C7: AccessibilityService shall not be used (Play Store restrictions and larger attack
surface).

2.6 Assumptions and dependencies
A1: Users grant required permissions.
A2: ML Kit Face Detection (bundled model) is available offline.
A3: Distance estimates are approximate (target error within about 15 percent after
calibration).
A4: A parent sets up child profiles; the child does not have the parent PIN.

3.3 Non-functional requirements
Performance and efficiency
NFR-P1: Per-frame detection latency shall be 100 ms or less on a mid-range device.
NFR-P2: Eye Guard response (from crossing threshold to dim shown) shall be 1 second
or less.
NFR-P3: Adaptive frame rate: about 1 fps when idle, up to 5 fps near thresholds or
when multiple faces are suspected.
NFR-P4: Monitoring shall run only while the screen is on and unlocked.
NFR-P5: Added battery drain should be measured and reported (target under 5
percent per hour of active monitoring; to be validated, not yet measured).
NFR-P6: App memory footprint should stay under 150 MB.
Reliability
NFR-R1: The service shall restart cleanly after crash, process death or reboot (with
user consent for autostart).
NFR-R2: If the camera is unavailable or in use by another app, the system shall degrade
gracefully and inform the user.
NFR-R3: The app shall guide users through OEM autostart and battery-optimisation
exemptions.
Usability
NFR-U1: First-run setup (permissions plus calibration) shall take under 3 minutes.
NFR-U2: Warnings shall be non-punitive and clearly explain why the screen dimmed.
NFR-U3: The camera-active indicator and foreground notification shall never be hidden
or suppressed.

5. Security and privacy requirements
5.1 Privacy by architecture
SEC-P1: The manifest shall not declare INTERNET or ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE ; "no
cloud" is therefore verifiable, not a promise.

Page 10 of 15

VisionGuard - Software Requirements Specification

SEC-P2: Frames shall be processed in memory only and never persisted, cached,
logged or shared.
SEC-P3: Only derived events shall be stored.
SEC-P4: The app shall provide a "Privacy Ledger" screen listing exactly what is stored,
plus a wipe control.
SEC-P5: allowBackup shall be false and data-extraction rules shall exclude all app
data.
SEC-P6: Explicit consent shall be shown before enabling camera analysis, with parental
consent flow for child profiles.

5.2 Data protection and authentication
SEC-D1: Database encryption with keys held in Android Keystore (StrongBox where
available), using Room with SQLCipher or Tink. Deprecated
androidx.security:security-crypto shall not be used.
SEC-D2: PINs shall be hashed with PBKDF2 or Argon2 using a unique salt, with attempt
rate-limiting and lockout back-off.
SEC-D3: Parent actions shall support BiometricPrompt with a CryptoObject .
SEC-D4: FLAG_SECURE shall be set on dashboard and settings screens to prevent
screenshots and screen recording.

5.3 Application hardening
SEC-H1: All components shall be exported="false" unless required.
SEC-H2: usesCleartextTraffic="false" and a restrictive network security config
shall be set for future-proofing.
SEC-H3: R8 minification enabled and release builds properly signed; debug flags off.
SEC-H4: Dependencies shall be scanned (Dependabot or OWASP Dependency-Check)
and an SBOM generated.
SEC-H5: Settings shall be PIN-protected; Device Admin uninstall protection is a
roadmap item.
SEC-H6: Overlay and block screens shall protect against tapjacking by not relying on
touch events from untrusted windows for sensitive actions.

5.5 Legal and compliance note
Processing facial imagery and monitoring minors' usage may trigger consent obligations
under the DPDP Act 2023 (India), COPPA and GDPR. Local-only processing reduces
exposure but does not remove these obligations. Qualified legal review is required before
any public release. This SRS is not legal advice.

7.3 Roadmap
1. Full PolicyEngine and persistence for Time Tokens
2. Keystore-backed database encryption and complete MASVS checklist
3. Automatic calibration and iris-based distance
4. Trusted-viewer mode for Privacy Guard
5. Device Admin uninstall protection and OEM battery handling
6. Optional local-first parent-to-child sync (Nearby Connections or mDNS with mutualauth TLS or Noise, end-to-end encrypted, no central server)
7. Play Store declarations (camera FGS, overlay, usage access) and legal review
